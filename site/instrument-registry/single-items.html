Single-item measures

A single question is what an organisation can field every month. This page gathers every single-item record in the registry, what each has been shown to screen for, and the published correspondence with the full instrument.

A single item cannot carry internal consistency or structural validity; those properties are category errors for it and are recorded as not applicable, never as absent. What a single item can carry, and what these records grade, is test-retest reliability, criterion validity and convergent validity against a multi-item instrument for the same construct. Where a published study reports that correspondence, it is recorded as a relation with the study cited: screens for where the study reports sensitivity and specificity against the instrument's threshold, corresponds with where it reports a correlation only (convergence, which is not a screening claim), and short form of where the item was derived from the instrument.

The registry records that a single item has been shown to screen for a construct; it never says that an organisation should use the item instead of the instrument, and it never states a threshold for action. Those are the implementer's decisions and outside the registry.

Work Ability Score (WAS), single-item

What it claims to measure: Global self-assessed current work ability relative to lifetime best.

  • short form of Work Ability Index (WAI). Single item against the full index: Spearman rs = 0.63 in 12,389 workers (El Fassi 2013); convergent validity of the single item confirmed in Swedish working samples (Ahlstrom 2010). Head-to-head, the single item discriminates less well than the full index for future sickness absence: AUC 0.67 against 0.78 (Roelen 2014). Recorded as fact; the registry does not say which form an implementer should use.
test-retest: Lowcriterion, reference standard: Absentcriterion, organisational: Moderateconvergent: Moderate

Single-item overall job satisfaction (Wanous tradition)

What it claims to measure: Global (overall) job satisfaction, that is a person's summary evaluative judgement of their job as a whole, captured in one question rather than by aggregating facet items.

  • corresponds with Multi-item overall job satisfaction scales (pooled across scales) (no registry record yet). Meta-analytic correlation between single-item and multi-item overall job satisfaction measures: r = 0.63 uncorrected, 0.67 corrected, N = 7,682 (Wanous 1997); single-item facet measures against the Job Descriptive Index facets 0.60 to 0.72 (Nagy 2002). No single multi-item job-satisfaction scale has a registry record yet; the relation targets the pooled multi-item literature and will be re-pointed when one is admitted.
test-retest: Absentcriterion, reference standard: Absentcriterion, organisational: Lowconvergent: High

Single-item perceived stress measure (Elo tradition, SISQ)

What it claims to measure: Perceived psychological stress / stress symptoms, that is subjectively experienced tension, restlessness, nervousness, anxiety or difficulty sleeping attributed to worry, captured in a single global self-rating.

  • screens for General Health Questionnaire-12 (GHQ-12). The single-item job-stressfulness measure against GHQ-12 caseness: sensitivity and specificity at or above 70 percent at a cut-off of 4 or more on the 5-point scale in high-strain occupational groups, N = 20,658 (Houdmont 2021). The validation is of the job-stressfulness wording, not the general stress-symptom wording on this record; the two are close variants. The record notes the wording distinction.
test-retest: Lowcriterion, reference standard: Lowcriterion, organisational: Lowconvergent: Moderate

Single-item fatigue measure (Van Hooff)

What it claims to measure: Momentary (state) subjective fatigue, that is how tired or fatigued a person feels at the moment of measurement, as distinct from chronic fatigue or need for recovery.

  • corresponds with Profile of Mood States, fatigue subscale (no registry record yet). Single-item fatigue against the POMS fatigue subscale: r = 0.80 in a Dutch academic-staff diary sample, n = 120 (Van Hooff 2007). Single sample, not replicated; the POMS has no registry record.
test-retest: Absentcriterion, reference standard: Absentcriterion, organisational: Absentconvergent: Low

Single items still to enter

Stage two of the field adds the well-evidenced single items that have been validated against a multi-item instrument for the same construct: the single-item burnout measure (validated against the Maslach emotional-exhaustion scale) and the single-item work-engagement measure (validated against the UWES). Each enters through the same admission rule; single items have exactly the measurement-property literature the rule asks for.

The four ONS-4 items are single items administered as a set; they are recorded as item records of ONS-4 and carry their evidence at the set level.

OWHS Instrument Registry, dataset version 0.9.0 (schema 0.7), generated 2026-09-03; grades last confirmed 2026-09-03 under rubric v1.6. A resource maintained under the Open Workplace Health Standard, distinct from the normative specification. Grades are editorial assessments of published evidence for a working-adult audience in any country; they are not part of the standard and change through the registry's public corrections process, not by RFC. Grades are single-rater and frozen from first publication until independent raters join (see who graded this).
