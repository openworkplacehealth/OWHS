Evidence sweep · November 2026

cycle 2026-11 · publication window from 1 October 2026 · filed 19 November 2026 · registry v0.9.0, citations appended in place

This is the November 2026 report for the instrument registry. Headline: five citations added on three records (PHQ-9, UWES-9 and CBI), and thirteen cells marked for a rater's review. No grade moved. The five studies did not come from the harvest. They were found by a web-search sweep run by an assisted session in October and November, and each was checked against Crossref and read at abstract level on 19 November before it was added. The harvest's own candidates were not screened this month.

The harvest

The planned run on 1 November (run 41307866511, issue #64) searched publication dates from 1 October to 1 November 2026, and works first indexed in Europe PMC from 3 August to 1 November 2026. It ended partial: 2 of its 289 expected channels did not complete, the OpenAlex citation channels for GAD-7 and PHQ-9, because keyless use ran out of its daily allowance. Those two instruments were not fully searched by that run. It listed 2,190 candidates: 595 normal priority and 1,595 low priority (no property term found in title or abstract), and 25 possible new instruments.

After an OpenAlex key was added, the planned November cycle was run again on 19 November (run 44581601008). This run completed: all 289 expected channels finished. It covered publication dates from 1 October to 19 November 2026 and works first indexed in Europe PMC from 21 August to 19 November 2026. The workflow rewrote issue #64 to describe it. It listed 3,812 candidates: 921 normal priority and 2,891 low priority, and 50 possible new instruments. The downloaded file's SHA-256 matches the one recorded in the issue. Apart from a search for the five studies below, its candidates were not screened in this report. The registry's cycle check (tools/cycle.py verify) does not yet accept the run, because it does not recognise the workflow as the issue's author. So the watermark was not advanced, and the next planned window still starts on the first day of the previous month.

Reading done: Listed 2,190 candidates in the 1 November run (595 normal priority, 1,595 low priority) and 3,812 in the 19 November run (921 normal priority, 2,891 low priority). Titles read for 0. Abstracts read for 0. Full texts read for 0. Not read: all of them. Both files were searched only for the five studies below, by DOI, author name and a phrase from each title. None of the five is in either file.

How the five studies were found, and why the harvest did not list them

A separate monthly assisted session searched the web on 1 October and 1 November and proposed these five studies. It could not reach the bibliographic indexes directly. On 19 November each DOI was resolved, its Crossref record was checked for title, authors, year and journal and for any correction or retraction notice (none was found), and its abstract was read: three from the abstract the publisher deposited with Crossref, two from the publisher's own page. No full text was read. Five of five were confirmed and none was dropped.

None of the five is in either November candidates file. That is a recall gap and is named here. All five were published before the November publication window opened on 1 October (between November 2025 and 28 September 2026). Two were first indexed in Europe PMC before either run's first-index window opened (3 August and 21 August). One is not in Europe PMC at all. The run that could have seen them was October's, which included a pass over the full publication history and did not complete (see the October report). Whether a complete quarterly run would have listed them has not been tested.

What was found

Figures below are from the abstracts that were read.

  • PHQ-9: Flores-Kanter and colleagues, BMC Psychiatry, January 2026 (10.1186/s12888-026-07820-9). An Argentine sample of 5,950 people in three subsamples: online (4,803), people seeking psychological help (98) and a random telephone sample (1,049). Confirmatory factor analysis and exploratory structural equation modelling supported one factor; two-factor models fitted slightly better but their factors correlated above 0.80. The one-factor model was invariant across gender, age groups and mode of administration. General-population sample with a small clinical subsample. Marked for review: structural validity; measurement invariance; populations, languages and norms.
  • PHQ-9: Greškovičová and colleagues, Scientific Reports, April 2026 (10.1038/s41598-026-47525-3). 2,893 Slovak helping professionals. One-, two-, four-factor and bifactor models all fitted acceptably to excellently; the bifactor analysis showed essential unidimensionality. Invariance across gender at configural, metric, scalar and strict levels. Scores were associated with anxiety, self-criticism, self-reassurance, compassion fatigue, compassion satisfaction and burnout. Working adults. Marked for review: structural validity; convergent and discriminant validity; measurement invariance; populations, languages and norms.
  • UWES-9: Heveri and colleagues, Scientific Reports, November 2025 (10.1038/s41598-025-26907-z). 707 Czech employees. A modified three-factor model fitted best (CFI 0.987, TLI 0.976, RMSEA 0.077, SRMR 0.017). Invariance across gender at configural, metric, scalar and strict levels. Positive associations with extraversion and self-efficacy and a negative one with neuroticism. Alpha 0.96, omega 0.96. Higher scores for professional staff, managers and people with more education. Working adults. Marked for review: structural validity; convergent and discriminant validity; internal consistency; measurement invariance; populations, languages and norms.
  • UWES-9: Nosáľová and Dudová, Studia Psychologica, September 2026 (10.31577/sp.2026.03.948). The Slovak version in two samples of employees (532 and 985). The three-factor structure was supported in both. Internal consistency was high and test-retest analysis (353 people) showed good stability; the abstract gives no coefficient for either. Invariance across gender in both samples and across manual, non-manual and management groups in the second. Negative association with burnout, positive with work performance and with another engagement measure. Working adults. Marked for review: structural validity; convergent and discriminant validity; internal consistency; test-retest reliability; measurement invariance; populations, languages and norms.
  • CBI: Amaya-Arias, Journal of Health Psychology, online November 2025, issue dated 2026 (10.1177/13591053251387819). 510 Colombian healthcare workers. Omega 0.89 to 0.92. The original structure was not fully confirmed: item 13 loaded weakly, and after removing it the model fitted adequately (CFI and TLI at least 0.95, RMSEA at most 0.05). Item response analysis showed adequate discrimination and ordered thresholds. Working adults. Marked for review: structural validity; internal consistency; populations, languages and norms.

What was not searched or not read

  • None of the candidates from either run was read. Screening them is still to do.
  • The OpenAlex citation channels for GAD-7 and PHQ-9 did not complete in the 1 November run. They completed in the 19 November run, whose candidates are also unread.
  • Works published in September 2026 fell in October's window. In November they were searched only through the Europe PMC first-index catch-up.
  • OpenAlex's update-date filter needs a premium key and is listed in every plan as unavailable.
  • Non-indexed and non-English journals, instrument manuals and conference literature remain blind spots. Silence on an instrument means nothing was read for it this month, not that nothing exists.

Grades

No grade, status, evidence form, indirectness flag, licence field or review date changed. Grades stay frozen until independent raters have joined. Marking a cell for review means a citation was added that a rater should read. It does not mean the grade is wrong.
