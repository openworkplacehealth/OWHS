How the monthly automation runs

the maintenance engine · updated 1 September 2026

The registry's central promise is that it stays current. This page explains exactly how, including what the automation is allowed to do, what it is forbidden to do, and what happens when it fails. It is written so a reader can audit the process rather than take it on trust.

The monthly cycle

  1. Sweep. On the first of each month, an automated literature sweep searches the published research for new psychometric evidence on every instrument the registry tracks: validations, reliability studies, invariance findings, responsiveness data, new populations and languages, licence changes, and genuinely new instruments. Citations are verified against independent sources before anything is reported. The sweep runs on PubMed and Europe PMC in parallel with four published search strings; the venues, the strings and their tested volumes are on the methods page, so the coverage can be checked and improved by anyone.
  2. Propose. The sweep writes a dated report and prepares a new version of the registry dataset: new citations attached to the affected records, last-reviewed dates refreshed on everything checked, and a changelog entry. The changes are opened as a public proposal, visible in full before anything is applied.
  3. Human review. A person reviews and merges the proposal. The automation cannot merge its own work. Once merged, the site rebuilds so the public last-reviewed dates and the download files advance together.
  4. Publish. The month's findings appear as a sweep report page, and the version lands in the changelog.

What the automation may and may not do

May do freelyMay never do
Add citations to a record, with the evidence attachedChange an evidence grade (it may flag one for human review, nothing more)
Refresh last-reviewed dates on records it checkedDelete or weaken existing evidence
Flag licence-page changes for manual verificationRewrite a licence status (only a human, checking the steward's live page, may do that)
Propose new watchlist candidatesAdd an instrument to the registry (admission needs independent evidence and a human decision)

Most months should see citations added and no grades move. A registry whose grades moved every month would be measuring its own search noise, not the literature.

When it fails

The failure mode this design guards hardest against is silence. The sweep notifies its operator on success and on failure; a separate monthly check verifies that a sweep proposal actually exists for the month; and any change to the automation itself is tested against a frozen month of known input with known-correct output, so quiet drift in its behaviour is caught rather than discovered. This caution exists because the project has lived the alternative: an early version of the sweep failed silently for a month, and the fix was rebuilt into the process rather than patched over.

Planned, and stated as planned

Two additions are designed but not yet running, and this page will say so until they are: a weekly deterministic harvester querying the open scholarly indexes directly, so coverage never depends on search luck; and an automated weekly check of every instrument's licence page against an archived copy. When they go live, this page changes and the change is dated.

The blind spots, stated

The sweep reads the major indexes and verifies what it cites. It will miss: journals not indexed in the sources it queries, non-English-language literature, instrument manuals and technical supplements, conference proceedings and theses. Silence on an instrument in any month means nothing corroborated was found, not that nothing exists.
