Evidence maintenance: current process and planned automation

the maintenance engine · updated 19 November 2026

This page distinguishes the evidence-maintenance process used so far from the automation still to be implemented. Reports and proposed changes are open to inspection. An automated search is not a judgement about an instrument, and finding no new evidence does not establish that none exists.

Current process

The September 2026 sweep used web search because its execution environment blocked direct access to PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref and publisher pages. It produced a report and a proposal that was reviewed and merged. The registry pages were then regenerated by hand. The September report records the published results. The proposal itself was not in the public repository, and this cycle does not establish a running public end-to-end update pipeline.

Four scheduled workflows now run in the public repository and query the open indexes directly: a licence-source watcher, weekly since 7 September 2026, and since October a monthly harvest of candidate literature from Europe PMC, OpenAlex and Crossref, a monthly retraction watcher and a monthly tripwire that checks the harvest ran and completed. Their run logs are public, and what each may write is set out in the evidence README. No harvest cycle has yet been verified complete. The 1 October run reached GitHub's six-hour limit and was cancelled with nothing written. The 1 November run finished in 29 minutes but ended partial, because OpenAlex, used without a key, refused to page past the first page of results for the two citation searches with more than 200 results.

Citations and review flags may be proposed for human review. Grades and statuses remain frozen, and licence classifications require source verification and a recorded decision. The public changelog records accepted changes. Regeneration of the registry pages on merge is not yet automatic.

What the automation may and may not do

Permitted proposalsProhibited automated changes
Add a citation with its source and flag the relevant cell for reviewChange a grade, status, evidence form or population judgement
Record the scope and date of a completed searchTreat a search run as human confirmation of a grade
Flag a change to a licence sourceChange a licence classification
Suggest an instrument for considerationAdmit an instrument without the published review process

A search run does not authorise a grade change. New evidence is reviewed through the public corrections process. Findings that could affect a grade, status or licence classification are referred for human review through the public corrections process; automated sweeps do not apply these changes.

When it fails

The failure mode this design guards hardest against is silence: an early version of the sweep failed silently for a month in 2026, and nobody noticed until the next run. The tripwire now runs on the third of each month and opens an issue when it cannot verify that month's harvest. An issue alone was not enough: the October and November ones went unread for weeks. The tripwire's issue, and the harvest's own issue when a cycle is not complete, is now assigned to the steward on GitHub with a mention, so GitHub emails a person. Its title names the month and the one action needed, and it says until when the month can be recovered; once that date has passed, it says the month can no longer be recovered.

The harvester prints its progress channel by channel and works to a time budget inside GitHub's limit, so a long run stops, writes what it found and says what it did not reach, instead of being cancelled with nothing. A missing or partial report must be treated as an incomplete maintenance cycle, not as a month with no new evidence. A replay fixture using saved source responses is not yet built.

The watchers say what they could not check. The licence watcher reports each page as changed, unchanged or not checked. Six pages refuse automated requests every week; they are listed as needing a person on every run and in a monthly reminder, and are never reported as unchanged. The retraction watcher names, on every run, the two cited DOIs that are registered with DataCite rather than Crossref and so cannot be checked at Crossref.

Planned, and stated as planned

Planned work comprises a measured historical retrieval check, automatic proposal creation, registry regeneration on merge and a saved-response regression fixture. The quarterly full-history search also needs a decision on how it should run: under the current plan it does not fit in one run, and its largest searches return more results than a run collects. Each capability will be described as running only after its workflow and run log are available here. Search coverage will remain limited by the sources, queries and access available.

The blind spots, stated

Web search and scholarly indexes have different coverage. Relevant work may be missed because of indexing, terminology, language, access restrictions or unavailable manuals and technical reports. Source failures and uncompleted searches must be reported separately from completed searches that found no candidates.
