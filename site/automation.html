Evidence maintenance: current process and planned automation

the maintenance engine · updated 5 September 2026

This page distinguishes the evidence-maintenance process used so far from the automation still to be implemented. Reports and proposed changes are open to inspection. An automated search is not a judgement about an instrument, and finding no new evidence does not establish that none exists.

Current process

A scheduled, assisted session runs on the first of each month. It reads the current dataset, searches for new psychometric evidence on every instrument the registry tracks, writes a dated report, and opens the proposed changes as a public proposal that a person reviews and merges; it cannot merge its own work. The September 2026 run used web search because its execution environment blocked direct access to PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref and publisher pages. It filed its report and opened a proposal, which was reviewed and merged, and the registry pages were then regenerated by hand. The September report records the published results; it should not be read as evidence that the planned direct-database pipeline ran.

Citations and review flags may be proposed for human review. Grades and statuses remain frozen, and licence classifications require source verification and a recorded decision. The public changelog records accepted changes. Regeneration of the registry pages on merge is not yet automatic.

What the automation may and may not do

Permitted proposalsProhibited automated changes
Add a citation with its source and flag the relevant cell for reviewChange a grade, status, evidence form or population judgement
Record the scope and date of a completed searchTreat a search run as human confirmation of a grade
Flag a change to a licence sourceChange a licence classification
Suggest an instrument for considerationAdmit an instrument without the published review process

A search run does not authorise a grade change. New evidence is reviewed through the public corrections process. Since 5 September 2026 the session's instructions forbid any change to a grade, status or licence field outright: a finding that looks as though it would move a grade is written up for human re-adjudication and nothing is changed.

When it fails

The failure mode this design guards hardest against is silence: an early version of the sweep failed silently for a month in 2026, and nobody noticed until the next run. The session is now instructed to notify its operator on success and on failure. The planned failure monitor will check that a monthly sweep produced the expected report or proposal and will raise a visible alert if it did not. The monitor and a replay fixture using saved source responses are not yet running. A missing report must be treated as an incomplete maintenance cycle, not as a month with no new evidence.

Planned, and stated as planned

Planned work comprises direct scholarly-index retrieval, a reproducible query file, a measured historical retrieval check, automatic proposal creation, registry regeneration on merge, a monthly failure monitor, a saved-response regression fixture, and licence and retraction watchers. Each capability will be described as running only after its workflow and run log are available here. Search coverage will remain limited by the sources, queries and access available.

The blind spots, stated

Web search and scholarly indexes have different coverage. Relevant work may be missed because of indexing, terminology, language, access restrictions or unavailable manuals and technical reports. Source failures and uncompleted searches must be reported separately from completed searches that found no candidates.
