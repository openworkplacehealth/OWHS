# How an instrument enters this registry

*Open Workplace Health Standard, instrument registry. Admission rule version 1.0, 2 September 2026, drafted with external review. Licence CC BY 4.0.*

The registry does not aim to list every measurement instrument in existence. It aims to cover the instruments a UK employer, or a vendor acting for one, might actually field, with one or two instruments per construct family, each added properly or not at all. This page states how an instrument gets in, who may propose one, and what happens to proposals we do not accept.

## Where the current list came from

The registry began with the instruments the OWHS question bank draws items from, so that every published item has an evidence record behind it, together with the instruments in common UK workplace use, one or two per construct family. That start, set out in a scoping note of 12 July 2026, was a working list, not a judgement about everything else. It was seeded from the question bank's sources and from common UK vendor practice, which skews towards wellbeing indices, engagement scales and clinical screeners and away from the occupational-epidemiology instruments that carry most of the published evidence against recorded work outcomes. The Job Content Questionnaire and the Effort-Reward Imbalance questionnaire are absent for that reason, and the registry's founding finding, that organisational criterion validity is absent or thin for most of the instruments graded, is partly an artefact of it.

## Three doors

**The question bank.** Where the OWHS question bank draws items from an instrument, that instrument gets a registry record. This is automatic and needs no proposal: no published item should be without an evidence record behind it.

**A proposal.** Anyone may propose an instrument as a public issue on the repository's instrument proposal template. The steward proposes on the same template, in public, with the same interest declaration. Until the repository opens with the public release, proposals reach the steward by email at hello@openworkplacehealth.org and are published with the repository.

**The monthly evidence sweep.** Where the sweep surfaces an instrument that meets the threshold below, it is proposed on the same public template so that the proposal, the check and the decision are on the record in the same way as anyone else's. The sweep never adds a record itself.

## What a proposal must contain

1. **The instrument**, named as its steward names it, with the version or form intended for the record.
2. **The construct**, mapped to a construct-domain code in the OWHS code list, or accompanied by a domain proposal where no code fits.
3. **The evidence**, cited by DOI: two peer-reviewed studies reporting a measurement property of this instrument in working-age samples, or one in UK working adults. Studies that use the instrument without reporting a measurement property of it do not count.
4. **The relevance**: either evidence that the instrument is in use with UK employers, vendors or national surveys, or a statement that its construct family has no record in the registry yet. Where the family already has one or two records, the proposal states what this instrument adds that they do not.
5. **The licence position**: the steward's current terms page, or a statement that no steward and no instrument-level licence exist.
6. **An interest declaration**: any financial or professional interest the proposer has in the instrument. Instrument developers and copyright holders are welcome to propose their own instruments and are asked to say so.

Evidence that an instrument's scores relate to recorded organisational outcomes is welcome in a proposal and counts towards relevance. It never substitutes for the evidence in point 3: an instrument with a strong outcome literature and a thin psychometric base is admissible and will be graded as it is found.

## What we check, and what we do not

Our check at this stage is clerical. We confirm that the cited DOIs resolve, that the studies concern working-age or UK working-adult samples, that they report a measurement property of the named instrument, and that the licence position can be established. We do not assess the quality of the evidence at admission, because that is grading, and grading follows the rubric and the rater conditions set out in the grading rubric.

A proposal that passes the check is admitted to the watchlist. It is not a finding about the instrument that we have admitted it, and it is not a finding that we have not yet graded it.

## From proposal to graded record

**Proposed.** The proposal is a public issue, visible from the moment it is opened.

**Verified.** The clerical check has passed. The date is recorded on the record.

**On the watchlist.** The instrument has a record, and every property on it reads not assessed: we have not yet searched the literature, which says nothing about the literature. No grade of any kind appears, including provisional or indicative grades. The record restates why: grades are pending independent raters, not pending our opinion. Each watchlist record is also a specific ask for a rater, with the proposer's cited studies and nine ungraded properties on it.

**Queued.** Grading order is published and is organised by construct family, not by the order proposals arrived. Instruments the steward's own products select are graded last within their family. Where there is no scheduled grading date, the record says so.

**Graded.** A rater grades the record under a published rubric version, under the rater conditions in force at the time. The grades enter the matrix, and the record leaves the watchlist.

## Counting

Graded instruments and watchlist instruments are counted separately wherever the registry states a number. They are not added together into a single total.

## Capacity

The registry has one part-time rater and grades and statuses are frozen until two named independent raters have joined. Until the first grading cycle after the freeze lifts has run, the registry publishes no throughput figure and no admissions cap, because it would be a guess. The family cap above is the published limit on scope in the meantime.

## Proposals we decline

Declined proposals are listed publicly with the reason, from this set:

- **below-evidence-threshold**: the cited studies do not report a measurement property of this instrument in working-age samples.
- **construct-out-of-scope**: the construct is outside workplace health and wellbeing as the standard's code list defines it.
- **no-uk-workplace-relevance**: no evidence of use with UK employers, vendors or national surveys, and the construct family already has records.
- **family-already-represented**: the construct family has records and the proposal does not state what this instrument adds.
- **duplicate-of-existing-record**: the instrument, or the form proposed, is already covered.
- **licence-position-undeterminable**: the steward's terms could not be read and no positive finding of no-formal-licence could be made.
- **item-text-unobtainable**: the instrument cannot be described without reproducing licensed item wording, which this registry does not do anywhere.

A decline is a decision about a proposal against a published threshold. It is not a judgement about the instrument, and it is not a judgement about whether the construct matters at work.

## Conflict of interest

The steward of this registry builds commercial products that may implement OWHS. Three things follow, and each can be checked rather than taken on trust. The steward proposes instruments on the same public template as anyone else, with the same interest declaration. Every record carries a field stating whether the steward's own products currently select the instrument; that field is being completed for all existing records before any new instrument is admitted, and no instrument is admitted until it is. Instruments the steward's products select are graded last within their construct family, so that proposal order and commercial interest cannot set the order of our work. Once a year the registry publishes the overlap between its contents and the steward's product selections, so that the figure is visible rather than inferred.

## What the registry is, and is not, seeking

The registry grades evidence about instruments, never evidence about interventions. Evidence that an instrument's scores relate to outcomes an organisation records is in scope and has its own graded property. Evidence that using an instrument improves anyone's wellbeing is a different literature, about interventions, and this registry does not cover it.
