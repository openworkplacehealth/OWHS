# Open Workplace Health Standard (OWHS)

> An open data standard and a permanent open catalogue for workplace health. Status: early open specification (v0.2 draft), open to review. OWHS was created by Zak Fenton and is currently stewarded by Alltoogether. Its specification, schemas and supporting materials are published under permissive licences and are open to inspect, use, challenge and improve. If OWHS earns independent use and recurring contribution, its governance will move to a multi-stakeholder structure so that no single commercial organisation controls its future.

Conflict of interest: Alltoogether develops commercial products that may implement OWHS. The standard does not require use of any Alltoogether product, and the project publishes its contribution, decision and change processes openly.

Licences: specification prose and guidance CC-BY 4.0; schemas, codelists, examples, dataset structure and tooling Apache 2.0. Third-party instrument and survey-item wording is never ours to license and stays with its source under the terms recorded per item.

Two things, one project. The standard says what a workplace-health record is: entities, schemas and code lists for sickness absence, return to work, wellbeing measurement and benefit provision, anchored to ONS, HSE, the statutory fit note and SSP. The catalogue says what the measures inside it are worth and where they come from: an instrument evidence registry and a survey question bank, both versioned, corrected in the open and kept as an open, forkable public record. Each is published at three depths: an explainer, a working page, and the full verifiable source.

## What each part is
- The standard: entity definitions, JSON Schemas and code lists for workplace-health records. Normative. Changes only by the public change process.
- The instrument registry: an evidence record for each instrument used to measure workplace health and wellbeing, with graded psychometric properties, the recorded licence position for employer or vendor use, and citations. A resource maintained under the standard, not part of it. It describes instruments and never reproduces their items.
- The question bank: survey items with provenance. An item's wording appears only where it is verified verbatim against the primary source and licensed for redistribution.
- Sweep reports: the monthly evidence screening reports for the registry. They add citations and flag cells for review; they never move a grade.
- Schemas and code lists: the machine-readable parts of the standard, each published as raw JSON at a stable address.
- Changelog: a dated record of every change to the registry, the specification and the site.

## Instruments in the registry, with the recorded licence position for employer or vendor use
Licence positions are the registry's record of the steward's terms at the date stated, not legal advice. Grades are single-rater and frozen. The registry makes no ranking and no recommendation.

## Registry status, stated plainly
- All grades were assigned by one rater employed by the steward. Grades and statuses are frozen from first publication until two named psychometric raters who are not steward employees have joined.
- AI assists literature retrieval, screening, extraction and drafting; it never assigns a grade and never writes to the dataset except through a human-merged pull request.
- The registry publishes no ranking and no "best instrument". Grades are not comparable across instruments.
- The registry describes instruments; it never reproduces them. The question bank renders item wording only where it is verified verbatim and openly licensed.
